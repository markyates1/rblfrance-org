“To Hell With That”

By Gyles Longley C.B.E. M.C.

Appeal for the autobiography of Gyles Longley C.B.E. M.C., former Chairman and Honorary President of the Paris Branch.

The Royal British Legion Paris Branch — a special appeal: “To Hell With That”: Gyles Longley C.B.E. M.C., ex Chairman and now Honorary President of the RBL Paris Branch, has finally given in to the persuasion of his friends and colleagues and written his autobiography. His reaction on being offered a job in an electricity showroom provided the title to the book and signalled the start of an eventful career elsewhere. Military specialists will be most interested in his wartime experiences across North Africa and up through Italy, but there is also a fascinating view of life in England before the war and sidelights on pre-war Europe, including Austria and Germany at this critical period. After the war Gyles had (and still has) a prominent role in the British community in Paris, and his account of this will interest many.

“Remember”

By Graham Scott

Graham Scott, another active member of the RBL Paris Branch, has published this moving pictorial account. He says of it: a year in the making, while my photographs were not all taken in a year, I did travel widely. I also did a lot of research on the stories behind the photographs, finding out in the process what really happened to a member of my late mother’s cousin. Not all the colour photos are mine, there are contributions from Mrs Lyn Lyon and W. O. Dougie Lee RAF. There are also photos from the internet, and all contributors are acknowledged where possible. Both World Wars are covered, as is the Cold War and some of the conflicts that took place from 1945.

I feel that to simply remember is not enough, we need to ensure that the conditions that breed conflict are not allowed to germinate. The compassion and selfless courage of our armed forces continues to exist today just as much as it did in 1914-1918 and 1939-1945, or even in Korea. An RAF NCO Air Loadmaster, one of the four crewmen of a Chinook, is on record as saying he would rather die than have to explain to the mother of a badly wounded soldier that her son was not evacuated because it was too dangerous. He then set off on a flight where they knew the Taliban were in all probability waiting for them. Please remember that, as you look at the night shot of the Boeing Vertol Chinook and Agusta Westland Apache.
