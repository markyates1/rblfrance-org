The Comete Escape Line Event

Ceremony of 7-8 September 2007, Le Pays Basque

This weekend saw the celebration of the eighth anniversary of the Comète Escape Line trek between St-Jean de Luz in France and the village of Oiartzun in Spain. The motto of le Résau d’evasion Comète is “Pugna Quion Percutias” or Fight Without Arms, and commemorates the incredible achievements of the Belgium run organisation, started in 1941 by a young girl named Andrée de Jongh, “petite cyclone”, which enabled more than 750 people – mainly aircrew – to escape from Nazi occupied Europe and return to Britain. Shot down allied airmen who evaded capture were hidden and passed down the line from Brussels, through France and finally over the border in the western Pyrenees into Spain.

The weekend started with a series of ceremonies commemorating the passeurs and evadees at war memorials and cemeteries in St Jean de Luz, Bayonne and Anglet. Our RBL contingent, consisting of Jack Douay and his wife, Scott Goodall, Tom and Wendy Lewis and myself, joined the celebrations at the Anglet War Memorial, where we paraded our standard alongside that of Comète and 19 French ones, an excellent turnout, and laid a wreath. Following the ceremony we moved to the Mairie where wartime couriers Andrée Dumont, “Nadine”, and her sister Micheline Uguex, “Michou”, were presented with gifts from the Mayor. We then had a vin d’honneur and speeches in the lovely setting of the Mairie’s reception room. A small number of RAF evaders from 1942 and 1943 were present, along with Dot Collins, widow of the former pilot and evader Maurice Collins. In the evening we all enjoyed a cheerful Basque meal in Ciboure. We missed the British contingent from ELMS, the Escape Lines Memorial Society, who were not attending this year.

On the Saturday morning we assembled at the beautiful Ciboure cemetery and after a Basque dance of welcome paid homage at the graves of Fiorentino Goikoetxea and his helper Kattalin Aguirre. The hikers then enjoyed a breakfast provided by the commune of Socoa before marching up to Urrunge where we laid another wreath at the war memorial and Tom Lewis once again paraded our standard with the Comète one. Once again the weather was fine and sunny, although thankfully cooler than the 32 degrees of last year.

We hope that next year we will once again see our colleagues from ELMS and we will be hoping to get the RAFA standard present at what is very much a memorial trek for those very brave young pilots who made the crossing into Spain and back to England to fight again.

Richard Holworthy

September 2007
