Recent event:

Operation Frankton 2007

The 65th Anniversary commemorations of Op Frankton started on Frankton Quay on Wednesday 12 December. The Guest of Honour was HM Ambassador, Sir Peter Westmacott (see left).Unfortunately Lord (Paddy) Ashdowne, the Patron of the Special Boat Service, was unable to attend as planned as he had been called to Afganistan.

It was good to see some serving Royal Marines present, including 2 who had just paddled down the Gironde retracing the Cockleshell Heroes' route in a kayak of the period and wearing contemporary kit, and an excellent bugler who sounded a poignant last Post at all the ceremonies.

The parade was followed by a reception hosted by the Association.  The ceremony marking the deaths of Sgt Wallace and Marine Ewart by a German firing squad in front of the bunker at Château Magnol, Blanquefort, took place the next day on a cold, crisp morning.

Wreaths were laid by the Mayor of Blanquefort, the Préfet, Frankton Souvenir, the SBS and ourselves.  There were 2 RBL standards, ours and that of the Poole RM Branch, and Ken Napier carried the RNA standard.   

Fourteen of us, a mixture of RBL and RNA members and friends, then drove down to La Réole where we had an excellent lunch together at the Restaurant aux Fontaines, prior to the unveiling of a plaque to Lt Mackinnon and Marine Conway at the hospital where they were captured. They had reached La Réole en route to the station there, where they had hoped to catch a train to Toulouse en route to escaping to Spain, but Mackinnon had a wounded knee which needed attention. The wife of the doctor who had treated him was present at the ceremony. The story did not end happily. Hitler had ordered that all special forces captured were to be executed, and they were taken via Bordeaux to Paris where this order was carried out.  They never gave away the names of their comrades

On the next morning, 14 December, the action moved north to the unveiling of a plaque at Beaunac and the unveiling of another at the Château de Segonzac, St Genes de Blaye, at the starting point of the Frankton trail on the 15th December.
