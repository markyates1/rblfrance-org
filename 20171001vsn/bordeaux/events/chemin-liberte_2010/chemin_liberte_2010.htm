The Royal British Legion 

Bordeaux & S.W. France Branch

Report of past event

Le Chemin de la Liberté, St Girons, July & September 2010

Every year in July your Chairman John Allsop and Standard-Bearer Tom Lewis attend the various ceremonies in and around St Girons in the Ariège for the official hike along the Chemin de la Liberté organised by Scott Goodall.
This year was no exception, with over 80 people from all walks of life and of different nationalities participating in the four-day walk across the Pyrenees into Spain following the route taken by the escapees from the Nazis during the second world war. The weather this year was fiercely hot at the beginning but cooled later, and all of the hikers completed the course, to the great benefit of the charities they were supporting, particularly the RBL.

In September this year we paid a second visit in support of a team of eight women, all of exceptional achievement in their chosen fields (and one man, their personal trainer Gary Johnson). Here they are on Hampstead Heath before setting off for France

WO1 Michaela Barwick, Army Physical Training Corps, currently serving at Headley Court

Sally Kettle, rowed the Atlantic twice, author and motivational speaker
Jo Salter, the RAF’s first-ever female fighter pilot, an author and inspirational speaker
Kate Nesbitt MC, Royal Navy medic. Only the second female in the Armed Services to be awarded the Military Cross, in Afghanistan.
Suzanne Dando, former British Gymnastics champion, captain of the Moscow Olympics Gymnastics team, now a television broadcaster. Leader of the team.
Gp Capt Deb Barber, first woman to attain Group Captain rank in the RAF Air Traffic branch, which she now heads.
Lt Cdr Penny Clark RN, Olympic yachtswoman, 10th in Beijing Olympics.
Moira Cameron, 22 years in the Army and the first-ever female Beefeater at the Tower of London.

Their trek was inspired by Suzanne Dando after she had read a book about Nancy Wake, who as an SOE agent and Resistance fighter helped thousands of servicemen across the Pyrenees – she too had to escape the Nazis in 1943.
Suzanne’s aim was to remember women in conflict and to raise a significant sum for the RBL, which currently stands at over £27,000.
After arriving in St Girons they had a day of R&R and a thorough check of their equipment by Scott Goodall, who has done the walk many times and well knows the problems of carrying an over-weight rucksack when climbing the High Peaks; however the girls assured him that mascara and lipstick were essential items which weighed very little but definitely boosted their morale.
Following a visit to the Museum in St Girons

where the girls got a view of what was ahead of them in the excellent displays,
a reception was held at the Mairie and gifts were exchanged with the mayor, after which they enjoyed a first-class dinner followed by a good night’s sleep in a proper bed – their last for four days!
With their experienced guide they set off in good spirits on a lovely sunny morning, and we met them at the Barn on the Col de l’Artigue where the seventeen-year old Louis Barrau was shot.
After a short ceremony at which an RBL wreath was laid and following a picnoc lunch (where Gary’s massage skills were already much in demand) they set off into the mountains

We, the supporters said our goodbyes with many hugs and best wishes for a successful trip. Apparently our good wishes did not extend to the weather which, as so often happens in this part of the French Pyrenées, changed rapidly to wind and rain, so they were very grateful for their excellent clothing supplied by Berghaus and their sleeping-bags loaned from Mountain Equipment.
They eventually completed the route, realising how fortunate they were to have all the support of modern-day equipment and clothing, not to mention a guaranteed supply of high-energy foods.
