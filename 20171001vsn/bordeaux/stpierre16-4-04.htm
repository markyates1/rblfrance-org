The Royal British Legion 

Bordeaux & S.W. France Branch

Report of past event

ST PIERRE D'IRUBE 16th APRIL 2004

Five of us met for lunch on Friday 16th April at the Biarritz Casino, prior to attending the Souvenir Français ceremonies which pay homage to the dead of both Wellington's and Napoleon's armies on the 14th April 1814.

The turn out was less than usual, due in part to local members being away for Easter. Gerald Mason MBE represented HMGC who was in UK, the Mercers came down from Bordeaux, and Ken Napier MBE paraded the standard.

The ceremonies started at Le monument de l'Aigle in Bayonne under the charge of Mr Jean Majerus, President of the local branch of Souvenir Français. The number of standard bearers was up on last year as we had no clash with rugby matches, but the music was still recorded. Wreaths were placed by the local Mayor, the Bayonne mayor having been delayed at the last minute, by Gerald Mason for HMGC and by Richard Holworthy for the RBL. Following the wreath laying we had a minute's silence "pour les morts" of both nations followed by the French and British national anthems.

The French version of the history of the fatal sortie from the citadel, written with a degree of spin that would do Downing Street proud, was read by General Ancelin. It was appropriately punctuated by the sound of small arms fire from the range within the citadel, still occupied by the French army.

All the participants then went by convoy to the Guards Cemeteries at Laharie and Coumères, situated on wooded slopes just outside Bayonne. Once again the standards were paraded and wreaths were laid by General Ancelin, Gerald Mason and Richard Holworthy. These cemeteries are in two small compounds which have been looked after by the Bayonne municipality since 1989. The access path to the former is now just outside the perimeter fence of a new housing estate and is fairly rough going. The cemeteries themselves are well maintained, although the lettering on the tombstones is in need of refurbishment, and are worth a visit by anyone who is passing through. Both Queen Victoria and King Edward V11 visited the sites when staying at Biarritz.
