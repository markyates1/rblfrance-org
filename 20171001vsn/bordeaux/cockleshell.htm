OPERATION FRANKTON - The Cockleshell Heroes

Ceremony in Charente Maritime on 10 June 2004


In December 1942, five kayaks each containing two British Royal Marine Commandos, were released by the submarine HMS TUNA off the Atlantic cost near Montalivet.
Their aim was to penetrate 110 km up the Gironde and attack enemy ships in the port of Bordeaux. Three kayaks were lost on the four-day journey. But the two kayaks which reached Bordeaux mined and considerably damaged six ships.
Of the ten men, two were drowned, six were caught and executed and two escaped with the help of the Resistance and reached England via Spain.
The type of kayak used led to the operation also becoming known in English as the "Cockleshell Heroes Raid".

June 2002 saw the official opening of the French Grande Route following the track taken by the two successful escapers, Major Blondie Haslar and Mne Bill Sparks, from Blaye to Ruffec.
On June 10th this year around 100 English and French walkers followed the five sections of the route in perfect weather. Corporal A.F. Laver and Marine W.H. Mills, who were in one of the two kayaks that completed the attack, subsequently made their way as planned across country heading for RUFFEC, where they were to meet up with the French Resistance. They were, however, betrayed and picked up by the French police, still in uniform, at La Garde, a suburb of Montlieu. They were handed over to the Germans, and shot on 23 March 1943.

In an unique French event the Mayor of Montlieu-la Garde had their names inscribed on the official French war memorial and the plaque was formally unveiled in the presence two of Cpl Laver's sisters, a nephew, a grand niece, Mne Mills' younger brother and a strong representation from the RM Special Boat Service at Poole, who had also walked in the morning.

In the Mairie the Anglo-French Frankton Souvenir Group had mounted an excellent exhibition describing the operation.
