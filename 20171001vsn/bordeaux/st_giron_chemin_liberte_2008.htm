St Girons Chemin de La Liberte

9-14 July 2008


Le Chemin de la Liberté, or Freedom Trail, is based on the town of St Girons in the Departement of the Ariège, 100 kms south of Toulouse.

The trail retraces the mountainous part of the wartime Pat O'Leary Line, an escape route over the central Pyrenees into northern Spain.

This year I went down to St Girons with John Allsop and Tom Lewis, where we met up with Scott Goodall who organises the walkers from the UK, including 14 from the RBL.

We started with the usual wreath laying at the Resistance Memorial at Kercabanac on the evening of the 9th July, followed by a vin d'honneur at La Maison du Chemin de La Liberté, which was opened last year.

The next day was hot and sunny and we joined the marchers at the memorial to 19 year old passeur, Louis Barrau, at the Col d'Artigue where more wreaths were laid. The views from the flower strewn summer pastures were stunning, and the only sounds were those of the tinkling of the cow bells and birdsong.

A substantial picnic lunch was provided by the Barrau family and the villagers of Sentenac d'Oust. This year's chemin was dedicated to the memory of Réne, one of the two surviving Barrau brothers, who died in April.

Unfortunately for the marchers the weather did not hold and by the end of day 2 constant rain, heavy thunderstorms and hail were encountered, although the laying of wreaths and remembrance crosses at the Halifax memorial did take place; The forecast for the crossing of the frontier ridge was terrible, with temperatures down to freezing, and on day 3 the trek was abandoned for safety reasons and the party came off the mountains to continue their journey to Spain by bus.

R. Holworthy
