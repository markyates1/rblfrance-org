Bordeaux & S.W. France Branch

Report of past event

St Girons Chemin de La Liberte

5 July 2006

The celebrations for the Chemin de la Liberté or Freedom Trail started on the 5th July at the Resistance Memorial at Kercabanac, the site of the German checkpoint at the entrance to the forbidden zone which covered the length of the Pyrenees.

Here the President of the Chemin de la Liberte Association, Col Guy Seris, gave a brief description of the work of the passeurs who guided many hundreds of French citizens and allied aircrew across the mountains and wreaths were laid on behalf of the mayor of St Girons and by your Chairman.

This was followed by a reception in the Mairie at St Girons, those attending having to brave a violent hailstorm to get to the town hall.

We missed the company of Maurice Collins, a pilot who had done the crossing in 1942, who sadly died of cancer earlier in the year. His ashes were laid high on the Franco-Spanish border in a moving ceremony during the trek. His wife, Dot, daughter and son in law and two of his crew members, Tommy Brady and Ted Hitchcock, were among the support party. Tommy and Ted survived 101 bomber missions during the war as part of his crew.

The following day the support group met up with the marchers at the barn on the Col d'Artigue where Louis Barrau, a 19 year old passeur, was ambushed and shot by the Nazis having been betrayed. A wreath was laid on his monument and a picnic lunch was provided by the villagers. Luckily the rain held off and during the afternoon the cloud lifted to reveal some glorious views.

That evening the Mayor of Seix held a reception in his mairie and this was followed by an excellent paella meal in a local restaurant. The next three days saw lovely weather and the crossing was a success.

5 RBL members from the UK, including Terry Whittles, a trustee, took part having raised £500 each towards RBL funds.
