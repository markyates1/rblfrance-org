The Royal British Legion 

Bordeaux & S.W. France Branch

Report of past event

St Girons Chemin de La Liberte

10 July 2007

Freedom Trail 2007
 

 

For once the weather was kind to us and the marchers. The commemorations started on Tuesday 10 July with the long awaited opening of the Chemin de Liberte Museum in the old railway station in St Girons by the Sous Prefet of the Ariege.

The speeches were long and were in French, English, Spanish, French again and Catalan. They were followed by a medal presentation and  wreath laying by the Sous Prefet, the Mayors of St Girons and of Alos in Spain, and by what was announced as "The Royal British Company".  This was a new one to me, somewhat redolent of the East India Company, but our wreath was laid with due ceremony and I was told by one of the old French veterans that it was much appreciated.

We had 2 RBL standard bearers, our own Tom Lewis and Ralph Scott, the standard bearer for District  South West in UK. He was one of the team of 15 marchers from the RBL who had each raised £500 to take part. The ceremony was rounded off by a vin d'amitie and a buffet.

 

The next day we reassembled at Kercabanac to lay wreaths at the monument to the Passeurs and those who crossed the Pyrenees. Once again RAF veteran, Tommy Brady, and Dot Collins, the widow of Maurice Collins who had escaped over this line in 1943 and whose ashes had been spread on the Franco Spanish border last year, were present. The third member of their crew, Ted Hitchcock, was unable to attend due to ill health.

This time the Royal British Company were left in limbo as the master of ceremonies went into the last post before I had a chance to lay the wreath, but the mistake was quickly corrected and "Nos amis les Anglais"  placed the wreath before the playing of the Marseillaise and the song of the Resistance.  The Mayor then hosted a reception in the Mairie at St Girons.

 

On Thursday 12th our support group met up with the marchers at Col de L'Artigue to pay respects to the memory of Louis Barrau, a 19 year old passeur who was waiting in a barn to escort a group of evadees across the mountains. He was betrayed by a Spanish guide and shot by the Nazis as he attempted to escape, having refused to surrender as his father and uncle had both been arrested and deported  and he did not want the same fate. His stele (memorial) is placed alongside the barn.

The setting is in stunning scenery, surrounded by meadows full of wild flowers with wonderful views of the surrounding mountains. The villagers where the Barrau family lived provided a picnic lunch for all.  Over the next 3 days the marchers left civilisation to cross the ice fields of the peaks before descending into Spain.
